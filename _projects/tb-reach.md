TB REACH is IRD's flagship tuberculosis programme, funded by the Stop TB
Partnership (a WHO-hosted initiative) and delivered through IRD's partner
Indus Hospital, Korangi, and later through Interactive Health Solutions. It
ran in several waves from 2010 onward, each testing a different way to find
and hold onto TB patients, across five countries.

The first wave (see
[Mission Zindagi]({{ '/projects/' | relative_url }}#mission-zindagi))
equipped community health workers and General Practitioners with
GPRS-enabled phones. Health workers screened people in clinics and
hospitals, collected sputum samples and GPS coordinates for suspected cases,
and were paid mobile-based incentives, through services such as UBL Omni,
for case detection, sputum collection and successful treatment.

Wave 3, also called
[Sehatmand Zindagi]({{ '/projects/' | relative_url }}#sehatmand-zindagi),
paid conditional cash transfers to General Practitioners and community
mobilisers for case detection and case holding, with reporting and
event-alert modules. It ran separately in Pakistan, Bangladesh, Tajikistan
and Tanzania; the Pakistan deployment was the largest, adding its own
barcode generator for patient IDs, a data warehouse, a field-monitoring web
app, a sync tool for a third-party lab system, and a dynamic reporting web
app.

Wave 4 ran two deployments side by side: contact tracing of adult TB cases
and reverse contact tracing of paediatric cases in rural Sindh, Pakistan
(see
[TB REACH 4 (Pakistan)]({{ '/projects/' | relative_url }}#tb-reach-4-pakistan)),
and community mobilisation against TB among mine workers in Port Shepstone,
South Africa, known as
[MINE-TB]({{ '/projects/' | relative_url }}#mine-tb).

A sixth wave, in Bangladesh, built a Java web module and companion Android
app for childhood TB case management (see
[Childhood TB, Bangladesh]({{ '/projects/' | relative_url }}#childhood-tb-bangladesh)).

## Stack

Java, GWT, MySQL, native Android, OpenMRS.
