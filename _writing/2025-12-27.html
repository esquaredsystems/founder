--- title: "What Stays When the Tools Expire" date: 2025-12-27 permalink: "/writing/2025/december/what-stays-when-tools-expire/" category: "misc" description: "Two philosophies of career growth, and the one that outlasts your productive days." ---

As I was packing for my week-long trip to #Lahore to attend a colleague’s wedding, I started planning out the days. The first one was of course the wedding, but the very next line item my memory suggested wasn’t Bhajjay ke Paey, Badshahi Masjid, or Minar-e-Pakistan. It was meeting with Saqib Amjad. We used to be a team about 5 years ago and had worked together for over 4 years.

But why Saqib? I asked myself when reflecting during the journey. That’s something to be loved about traveling by road. You can make use of time in sharpening, disciplining, tuning your mind. The question “why Saqib” triggered a mechanism where random thoughts are refined into usable ideas and lessons. After spending some time with the question, it dawned on me.

You see, I’m old-fashioned. And now I say this not with humility, but with pride. Most of the people I have worked with have a different philosophy of career growth. Staying more than a couple of years at a single place makes you rusty; add new weapons to your armoury of skills; don’t sit in a comfort zone, etc. You know! The usual fast-food-like ideas overfeeding social media nowadays. I think I’m getting carried away. So, being old-fashioned, my philosophy is opposite, that is, to stick around till phase 2. Phase 1 is when you adopt the new culture. Phase 2 is you make meaningful contributions to the culture itself. Not to get satisfied until you have made a difference, not to yourself but to the individuals, the teams, the company you are with.

The first philosophy gives you quick financial growth, new tools, technologies and methods. The second builds you long-lasting relationships. Not relationships. Long. Lasting. Relationships. Saqib’s name was there on top because that’s what I primarily achieved. A strong bond.

I’m not saying one philosophy is better than the other. I’m only presenting my experience. Handsome paycheck, tools, techniques, these have a limited life. Would you not want something that outlasts your productive days?

Anyways, we did end up meeting. Nothing catchy for LinkedIn, not even selfies. Just two old friends resuming from where they left, complaining about the world, praising and criticizing random things, exchanging ideas and that sort.
